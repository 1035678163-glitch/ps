# Craft Reference — vocabulary banks and a worked example

Load this when you need concrete wording for a block, or a model of the finished form.

---

## Worked Example

**Request:** "Create an image for an AC repair scene with a symmetrical composition, viewed from inside the system. The technician should be centered in the frame and look photorealistic or cinematic movie style."

**Finished prompt** (10 blocks, ~400 words):

> **Frame.** A photorealistic documentary photograph of an HVAC technician at a commercial rooftop air handler, taken from inside the open equipment compartment looking outward. The eye enters along the copper line set at lower left and lands on his face, framed between the two vertical coil banks.
>
> **Moment.** He has just stopped turning the wrench and tilted his head toward the compressor, listening. His hand is still closed on the tool. He hasn't decided anything yet.
>
> **Light.** Late morning, early September, high thin cloud. The compartment interior is a dark box; the world outside is three stops brighter, so the daylight behind him rims his shoulders and the edge of his jaw. A clipped-on tungsten work lamp inside the compartment throws warm frontal fill on his face, and the galvanized panel bounces a little more. Key to fill about 3:1. The light idea: he is the only bright thing inside a dark machine, lit by the world outside it.
>
> **Camera.** Chest height, roughly 60cm back from the coil face, 35mm on a full-frame body at f/2.8, Black Pro-Mist ⅛ so the sky highlights bloom slightly. Sharp on his eyes and the wrench; the near coil fins are soft shapes; the rooftop behind him dissolves. Mild vignette, cat's-eye bokeh in the corners.
>
> **Composition.** Three planes: out-of-focus coil fins and a hanging service tag in the foreground, the technician in the midground filling about half the frame height, ductwork and pale sky behind. He separates from the ground by tone. Off-center by a hand's width, with lookroom toward the compressor he is listening to. The duct line clears the top of his head; nothing touches the frame edge.
>
> **Environment.** Galvanized panel edges, taped insulation, a refrigerant gauge set hooked over a bracket, coiled wire, a scuffed gasket, and a Gatorade bottle standing on the curb behind him where someone set it down and forgot it.
>
> **Subject truth.** Navy work shirt, sleeves rolled unevenly, left cuff darker with sweat, unbranded patch. Safety glasses pushed up into his hair. Asymmetrical expression — mouth slightly slack, one brow raised, eyes unfocused and aimed low toward the sound rather than at the camera. Left hand braced flat on the panel edge, right hand closed around the wrench. Weight on the back foot.
>
> **Imperfections.** Sweat sheen at the hairline and beside the nose, visible pores, a healing nick on one knuckle, fabric pilled at the elbow, galvanized dust hanging in the backlight, fingerprints and a grease smear on the panel, sensor noise in the compartment shadows, one blown highlight where the sky clips.
>
> **Punctum.** A faded cartoon sticker on the inside of the access panel, half peeled, at shoulder height — put there years ago by someone else.
>
> **Palette, grade & reference.** Near-monochrome cool industrial grey and galvanized silver, broken by one warm note: the work lamp on his face and the amber of his skin. Kodak Portra 400, low contrast, warm skin retained. It should look like an unretouched frame from a working photo essay on the building trades. No CGI appearance, no plastic or waxy skin, no beauty retouching, no over-sharpening, no HDR halos, no unmotivated rim lights, no artificial symmetry, no malformed hands, no watermark or text.

**What the request did not say:** the season, the light sources, the lens, the uniform, the palette, the instant, or the sticker. All inferred. That inference is the job.

**Note the one deliberate override.** The user asked for symmetry and centering. Perfect bilateral symmetry with a centered subject is on the cliché list, so the prompt keeps the *framing device* — coil banks on both sides — and offsets the subject by a hand's width. When a requested element fights the picture, keep the intent and adjust the execution, then say so in the assumptions line.

## Vocabulary Banks

### Moment (block 2)

The instant, not the activity. Useful shapes:

- **The beat before** — hand raised but not yet landed, mouth open before the word, foot off the ground
- **The beat after** — the follow-through, the exhale, the moment the tool comes away
- **The pause inside the action** — checking, listening, reading, waiting for a machine to respond
- **The unaware moment** — attention on something outside the frame
- **The transition** — turning, standing, setting down, stepping through
- **Reaction, not action** — photograph the person watching the thing, not the thing

Always name **what just happened** and **what is about to**.

### Light (block 3)

- **Natural:** golden hour rim light, blue hour ambient, overcast softbox sky, hard midday sun, dappled light through leaves, window light falling off across a room, bounce from a white wall, skylight top light
- **Practical:** bare tungsten work lamp, fluorescent tube with a green cast, monitor glow, headlights through fog, neon spill, phone screen underlight, firelight flicker, open refrigerator
- **Studio:** single softbox 45° camera left, beauty dish with under-fill, hard key with a black flag opposite, rim separation light, seamless backdrop falloff, north window
- **Reads as real:** mixed color temperature, visible falloff across a wall, one blown highlight, flare from an off-axis source, light wrapping an edge, a shadow with a soft edge near the subject and a hard one far from it
- **Ratios:** 1:1 flat and honest · 2:1 gentle modeling · 4:1 dramatic, shadow still reads · 8:1+ low key, falls to black
- **Light ideas:** a single shaft crossing a dark room · subject in shadow against a blown-out ground · rim only, face falling to black · one practical doing all the work · light from below (unnatural, uneasy) · silhouette with detail only in the highlights

### Camera & rendering (block 4)

**Bodies:** full-frame mirrorless (clean, neutral) · full-frame cinema camera (filmic latitude, gentle highlight roll-off) · medium format (extreme detail, shallow plane, commercial polish) · 35mm film with visible grain (reportage) · disposable with on-camera flash (harsh, hot foreground) · security camera or dashcam (low contrast, smear, timestamp) · large format view camera (near-to-far sharpness, corrected verticals)

**Rendering character — the anti-CGI lever:**
- Black Pro-Mist ¼ or ⅛, Glimmerglass — halation, lifted blacks, bloomed highlights
- Anamorphic — oval bokeh, horizontal blue streak flare, 2.39:1, mustache distortion
- Vintage uncoated glass — veiling flare, low contrast, gentle falloff
- 7-blade aperture — heptagonal specular highlights instead of perfect circles
- Cat's-eye bokeh at the frame edges; vignetting; chromatic fringing on hard edges
- Halation around bright sources on film; grain structure matched to ISO
- 1/48s at 24fps (180° shutter) for correct motion blur; rolling-shutter skew on fast pans

**Depth of field:** `f/1.4` face isolated, background dissolved · `f/2.8` subject sharp, environment legible · `f/5.6` group in focus · `f/11` near-to-far sharp · macro `f/8` with a 2cm plane

**Exposure decisions:** expose for the highlights and let the shadows block up · protect skin at Zone VI · let the window blow · retain detail only in the highlights (silhouette) · overexpose a stop for an airy high-key look

### Composition (block 5)

- **Layering** — something in each of foreground, midground, background. Depth comes from planes, not from bokeh.
- **Figure-to-ground separation** — by tone, by color, or by focus. Name which one.
- **Edge discipline / tangents** — nothing kissing the frame edge, no background line growing out of a head, no limb cropped at a joint. The most common amateur tell.
- **Negative space** — how much of the frame is empty, and where.
- **Lookroom / noseroom / headroom** — space in the direction of the gaze or movement.
- **Subject scale** — "the figure fills about a third of the frame height."
- **Obstruction** — shoot *through* something: glass, a doorway, foliage, steam, a shoulder. Instantly reads as witnessed rather than staged.
- **Vantage with access** — a position a stock photographer would not have had: under the work surface, from inside the machine, over the shoulder, from the floor.
- **Entry and exit** — where the eye enters the frame and where it leaves.
- **Diagonals** carry energy; **horizontals** carry calm; **frame-within-frame** carries containment.

### Environment (block 6)

Distribute across planes. Include at least one thing that does not serve the picture — a coat over a chair back, mismatched chairs, a charging cable, a bottle set down and forgotten, a printed notice taped crooked. Real photographs contain irrelevant objects; perfect relevance is a tell.

### Subject truth (block 7)

- **Micro-expression:** asymmetrical — one brow higher, mouth slightly open, mid-blink, mid-speech, the expression between two expressions
- **Gaze:** aimed at a *specific* thing in or out of frame, never vaguely at the lens unless intended. Dead, unfocused eyes are the second-biggest AI tell after perfection.
- **Hands:** always doing something specific. State the grip. Idle empty hands read uncanny.
- **Weight:** which foot, leaning on what, contrapposto, the shoulder that drops
- **Skin physics:** subsurface scattering through ears, nostrils and fingers held near light; sebum on the T-zone; specular sheen versus diffuse cheek
- **No person in frame → Materials & surfaces:** substrate, finish, age, and how light behaves on each — anisotropic streaks on brushed metal, Fresnel falloff at grazing angles, contact shadow where objects touch, caustics through glass, weave and drape under gravity

### Imperfections (block 8 — pull 5+, from three or more categories)

- **Skin:** visible pores, fine lines, uneven tone, stubble, flyaway hairs, chapped lips, sweat sheen at the hairline, sunburn on the neck
- **Fabric:** wrinkles at the elbow, pilling, a loose thread, faded knees, a stain near the cuff, a collar sitting slightly wrong
- **Air:** dust in a beam, haze, steam, breath in cold air, smoke drift *(only where the air would actually carry it)*
- **Surfaces:** fingerprints, water spots, scuffed edges, chipped paint, oxidized metal, scratched acrylic, crumbs, dried adhesive residue
- **Optical:** slight motion blur on a moving hand, one blown highlight, chromatic fringing, sensor noise in the shadows, a smudge on the lens
- **Environmental:** cable clutter, a coffee cup out of place, a wear pattern on the floor, mismatched chairs, a sticker peeling off a panel

### Punctum (block 9 — exactly one)

Not a flaw — a fact with a history. A child's drawing taped inside a service panel · a wedding ring over a work glove · one mismatched sock · a rosary on the rear-view mirror · a name scratched into a tool handle · a bandage on the wrong hand · a birthday card still standing on the shelf in March. It should be describable to someone else in one sentence, and it should be the thing they remember.

### Palette (block 10)

Name the **relationship** first, then the tones. Complementary (warm subject, cool ground) · analogous (a single family, harmony) · near-monochrome with one saturated break · split-tone (cool shadows, warm highlights) · muted with a single accent.

Starting points: Industrial — brushed metal, neutral gray, safety yellow, navy workwear, warm skin · Domestic morning — warm oak, cream linen, pale sky blue, brass, soft green · Clinical — cool white, stainless, pale mint, shadowless gray · Night urban — sodium orange, deep cyan shadow, wet asphalt black, neon magenta · Editorial food — deep umber, cream, olive, one saturated accent.

Film stocks and grades: `references/looks.md` §3.

### Realism directives (close block 10 — vary the wording)

"An unretouched frame from a photo essay." · "Indistinguishable from a photograph taken on assignment." · "A still pulled from a feature film, color-graded but not stylized." · "Shot for an annual report — competent and honest, not glamorous." · "A frame the photographer would have printed."

Always pair with the negative list: no CGI appearance, no plastic or waxy skin, no beauty retouching, no over-sharpening, no HDR halos, no artificial bokeh balls, no unmotivated rim lights, no extra fingers or malformed hands, no watermark or text unless requested.

## Scene Adaptations

| Scene type | Swap block 7 (Subject truth) for | Emphasize |
|---|---|---|
| Product / e-commerce | Materials & surfaces: substrate, finish, micro-texture, edge wear | Lighting shape, reflection control, contact shadow, 100mm macro, f/8 |
| Architecture / interior | Materials: flooring, wall finish, fixture metal, textile | Corrected verticals, time of day, occupied clutter, dusk balance |
| Food | Plating & serviceware: ceramic glaze, linen, utensil metal | Steam, crumbs, condensation, 45° or overhead, side light, one bite taken |
| Landscape | Terrain & vegetation detail | Atmospheric haze layers, sun position, f/11, foreground anchor, weather as event |
| Portrait | Keep clothing; add fit, condition, drape | Catchlights, skin texture, 85mm, single key plus fill, the gaze target |
| Street | Keep clothing; add what they're carrying | Obstruction, layering, the moment, available light only |

## Text Inside the Image

Most models mangle rendered text. If the image must contain legible words (a sign, a label, a UI), state the exact string in quotes, keep it under ~5 words, and run `--model pro` (Gemini 3 Pro Image) or `--model gpt` — both handle typography far better than the flash tier.

## Locking a House Style Across Many Images

Freeze blocks 3, 4 and 10 (or pick one preset from `references/looks.md`) and vary only 1, 2, 5, 6, 7, 9. Write the frozen blocks once into `style.txt`, then `cat style.txt shot.txt > prompt.txt` before generating. Faces will still drift between images — no prompt fixes that; that needs reference-image conditioning, which is a different endpoint than the one wired into `generate.sh`.
